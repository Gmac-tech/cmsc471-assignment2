About the data

  • Source: World Bank Gender Statistics (World Development Indicators extract, updated 2021-09-15). Labor force participation figures are ILO modeled estimates; the WBL index comes from the World Bank's Women, Business and the Law project.
  • Filtering: World Bank aggregates such as "World" and "Arab World" were removed so that only countries are counted. Labor force data ends in 2019, so the charts stop there.
  • Coverage: the country metadata lists 217 countries, but only 187 have labor force estimates for women and men, so the line chart covers 187. The scatterplot shows only countries that have both a labor force estimate and a legal score (178 in 2019); countries with only one of the two are excluded. Not all 217 countries are represented in either chart.
  • Historical legal scores: the World Bank's WBL series starts in 1970 and its historical scores were reconstructed retrospectively from past laws, not measured at the time (see the caution under the scatterplot).
  • Computation: gap = male rate − female rate for each country-year; regional values are medians, shaded bands are the 25th–75th percentile. The dashed line is a least-squares fit across the dots shown.
  • Limitations and bias: unpaid care work is not counted as labor force participation. The WBL index scores laws on paper, not enforcement. Medians treat small and large countries equally, and "North America" contains only two countries. A dot does not show causation: income, culture and economic structure also matter.
